## Answer
For initial Medicare coverage of a CGM, the beneficiary must have an in‑person or Medicare‑approved telehealth visit with the treating practitioner within 6 months before ordering the device[4]. For continued coverage, a follow‑up visit is required every 6 months after the initial prescription, also in‑person or via Medicare‑approved telehealth, to document adherence to the CGM regimen and diabetes treatment plan[2][9][14]. While some non‑CMS sources describe the follow‑up as assessing ongoing benefit and adjusting therapy[1][16], the LCD L33822 (the governing primary policy, effective 2024‑10‑01) specifies the visit’s purpose as documenting adherence, not therapy adjustment[2][7][14].

## Criteria / Details
- **Initial coverage:** Treating practitioner must have seen the beneficiary in person or through a Medicare‑approved telehealth visit within 6 months prior to ordering the CGM[4] (also noted by CoveredUSA[1], Doko MD[10], and danatech[13]). 
- **Continued coverage:** Every 6 months following the initial CGM prescription, the treating practitioner must conduct an in‑person or Medicare‑approved telehealth visit with the beneficiary[2][14]. 
- **Purpose of the 6‑month follow‑up visit:** To document the beneficiary’s adherence to the CGM regimen and diabetes treatment plan (and to support continued medical necessity)[7][9][14]. 
- **Alternative description (non‑CMS):** Some sources state the visit assesses ongoing benefit and adjusts therapy[1][16], but the LCD L33822 governs and specifies documentation of adherence as the required purpose[2][7]. 

## Codes and documentation
- A52464 limits CGM supply billing to a maximum of three (3) months (90 days) per claim[11]. 
- For each six‑month practitioner visit, the medical record must contain sufficient information to determine that the beneficiary continues to adhere to their diabetes treatment regimen and use of the CGM device[12]. 
- Documentation of the six‑month in‑person or telehealth visit is required every six months after the initial prescription[9]. 

## Effective dates relied on
- LCD L33822 – Glucose Monitors (L33822) – CMS, revision effective 2024‑10‑01[2][4][7][14][16]. 
- Article A52464 – Article – Glucose Monitor – Policy Article (A52464), revision effective 2025‑02‑18[9][11][12]. 

## Contradictions and caveats
- The LCD L33822 (effective 2024‑10‑01) states that the six‑month follow‑up visit must **document adherence** to the CGM regimen and diabetes treatment plan[2][7][14]. 
- A non‑CMS source (CoveredUSA) and a contradictory interpretation of the same LCD passage claim the visit must **assess ongoing benefit and adjust therapy**[1][16]. 
- Because the LCD is a primary policy document with a later effective date, it governs over the secondary source; therefore, the required purpose of the follow‑up visit is documentation of adherence, not explicit therapy adjustment. 
- The April 16, 2023 revision of LCD L33822 removed the insulin‑treated requirement but the evidence does not confirm whether it changed practitioner visit frequency[6][17]. 

## Unknowns / not verified
- Whether the April 2023 revision of LCD L33822 altered practitioner visit frequency requirements or left them unchanged could not be confirmed from the revision history entries reviewed. 
- Whether documentation of a new practitioner visit at the time of dispensing would permit billing or dispensing of CGM supplies beyond the 90‑day maximum established in A52464[11] remains unverified. 
- It could not be confirmed whether other non‑CMS sources (e.g., ADCES danatech, Abbott supplier materials, ACU‑Serve guidance) omit or differ on the initial‑visit‑within‑6‑months timing requirement, as those documents were not fetched. 
- Whether the LCD’s wording about “document adherence” implicitly includes any assessment of ongoing benefit or therapy adjustment is not settled by the evidence.
