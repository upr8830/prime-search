**Short answer:** it depends entirely on the payer, but under **traditional Medicare criteria, generally no** for a patient with type 2 diabetes who is on metformin alone and does **not** have documented problematic hypoglycemia.

Here is how major payers typically handle this:

| Payer / Program | CGM for metformin-only T2D? | Typical criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **Medicare (CMS)** | **Not covered** unless problematic hypoglycemia is documented | Must be **insulin-treated** **OR** have documented recurrent level 2 hypoglycemia (<54 mg/dL) despite med adjustments, or one level 3 event requiring assistance. The KX modifier must **not** be used for beneficiaries exclusively on oral agents like metformin. |
| **Commercial plans** | **Sometimes**, but often requires prior authorization | Policies vary widely. Some plans still require insulin therapy; others have begun covering non–insulin-treated T2D if glycemic targets are not being met or based on plan-specific InterQual/medical necessity criteria. |
| **Medicaid** | **Varies by state** | Many states limit CGM to insulin-treated individuals or type 1 diabetes. Some are broadening access, but criteria differ significantly. |

### Key details from current policies:
- **Medicare** explicitly states that beneficiaries treated **exclusively with oral hypoglycemic agents** do not meet standard therapeutic CGM coverage requirements unless they have a documented history of problematic hypoglycemia (recurrent level 2 events or a single level 3 event) ([CMS Policy Article A52464](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/article.aspx?articleId=52464), [Dexcom Medicare FAQ](https://www.dexcom.com/en-us/faqs/medicare), [Doko MD summary](https://doko.md/medicare-cover-cgm-type-2-diabetes)).
- **UnitedHealthcare** references 2024 evidence showing CGM improves glycemic control in non–insulin-treated type 2 diabetes, but coverage still depends on meeting plan-specific medical necessity criteria, often through prior authorization ([UnitedHealthcare policy PDF](https://www.uhcprovider.com/content/dam/provider/docs/public/policies/comm-medical-drug/continuous-glucose-monitoring-insulin-delivery-managing-diabetes.pdf)).
- **Over-the-counter CGMs** (e.g., Abbott Libre Rio) are now marketed specifically for adults with type 2 diabetes not using insulin, but these are typically patient-pay retail products and may not be covered by insurance as DME/pharmacy benefits ([BCBSRI policy](https://www.bcbsri.com/providers/medicalpolicies/files/glucose-monitoring-continuous)).

### Bottom line for your patient:
If this is a **Medicare** patient on metformin alone without qualifying hypoglycemia, you likely **cannot approve** a CGM under current Medicare coverage rules.  
If this is a **commercially insured** patient, you should check the specific plan’s prior authorization policy—coverage for non-insulin T2D is expanding but is not universal.
