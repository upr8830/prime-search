## Answer
This asks about a specific patient's coverage, so the answer below will be at the policy level rather than an individual coverage decision. 
Under current Medicare policy, a beneficiary with type 2 diabetes who uses only metformin (non‑insulin) may obtain coverage for a therapeutic continuous glucose monitor only if they have a documented history of problematic hypoglycemia; without such documentation they do not meet the coverage criteria. 
The alternative pathway is insulin treatment, which the patient does not have. 

## Criteria / Details
- Therapeutic CGM coverage requires either insulin treatment **or** a documented history of problematic hypoglycemia (see LCD L33822, revision effective 2024-10-01) {[6][7][12][15]}. 
- For non‑insulin‑treated patients, the hypoglycemia pathway demands documentation of at least one level‑2 or level‑3 hypoglycemic event as defined in the policy (see Clinicians! Are You Ordering Glucose Monitors..., revision effective 2026-04-01) {[1]}. 
- The beneficiary must have sufficient information in the medical record to confirm a diagnosis of diabetes mellitus (see Article A52464, revision effective 2025-02-18) {[13]}. 
- Documentation must show that the beneficiary is actually testing at a frequency that corroborates the quantity of CGM supplies dispensed (see Article A52464, revision effective 2025-02-18) {[3]}. 
- When supplied, up to a maximum of three months (90 days) of CGM supply allowance may be billed at a time (see Article A52464 coding guidelines, revision effective 2025-02-18) {[14]}. 

## Codes and documentation
- Documentation of hypoglycemia events must include glucose values <54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L) with either recurrent level‑2 events or a single level‑3 event requiring third‑party assistance (see Clinicians! Are You Ordering Glucose Monitors..., revision effective 2026-04-01) {[1]}. 
- Proof of diabetes mellitus must be present in the medical record (see Article A52464, revision effective 2025-02-18) {[13]}. 
- Supply‑frequency documentation can be a narrative statement or a copy of the beneficiary’s log showing testing frequency that matches dispensed quantities (see Article A52464, revision effective 2025-02-18) {[3]}. 
- Up to 90 days of CGM supply (codes A4238 or A4239) may be billed at a time (see Article A52464 coding guidelines, revision effective 2025-02-18) {[14]}.

## Contradictions and caveats
- The reviewer noted a contradiction where claim c18 stated that LCD L33822 still requires the beneficiary to be insulin‑treated, but the LCD actually reads “The beneficiary is insulin‑treated; or,” showing insulin treatment is only one alternative. The LCD therefore presents insulin treatment and problematic hypoglycemia as alternative criteria, not a sole requirement, and this 2024‑10‑01 revision governs {[6][7][12][15]}. 
- Dexcom ([4]) and Doko MD ([8]) are secondary/web explanations; primary policy documents (LCD L33822, Clinicians! article, Article A52464) take precedence when they conflict. 
- The April 2023 revision removed the “multiple (three or more) daily administrations of insulin” language from the insulin‑treated criterion but did not eliminate the insulin requirement itself {[10][17]}. 
- Claim c1 (non‑insulin‑treated patient must have a history of problematic hypoglycemia with documentation) is supported by the primary policy Clinicians! article {[1]}.
